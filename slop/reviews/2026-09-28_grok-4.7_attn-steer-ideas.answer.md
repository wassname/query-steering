The setup, as I read it: on Qwen3-4B and hybrid Qwen3.5-4B, “dom” is a last-token, pre-RoPE query mean-difference between blunt-honest and sycophantic system prompts, added at every position in every softmax layer. The test is different: the user states a wrong fact, and success is naming the truth without becoming contrarian or incoherent. Residual mean-diff on “correct answer is” / “as you said” pairs already hits the ceiling on Qwen3.5-4B. That is not evidence that residual beats attention. Pairs, site, and operator are confounded.

Observed, not inferred: dom saturates the hybrid (15/16) and stalls on dense Qwen3-4B (~10/16); layer subsampling does not close that gap; a secret-word query vector increased claim-copying; the poem bait got more flattering; Q-VJP toward the residual contrast is nearly orthogonal to q* and does not help; dom can work while lowering that probe. Inferred: q* is a persona/content bias, not “move h_final toward honest.” Under RoPE, a constant pre-RoPE add is a rotated content bias, not a fixed positional prior. Missing: same pairs through both operators; per-head and per-span attention on the claim; whether small-model failure is coherence or a missing direction.

I would not try to beat 15/16. The gap that can move is Qwen3-4B and the sub-2B models, with agree and a pre-set damage score held fixed.

Ideas, in the order I would actually run them:

1. Same operator, test-like pairs. Hold a neutral system prompt; contrast “user states wrong” vs “user states right,” or teacher-forced correct vs copied names, and take q at the answer token, not the prompt end. Apply as now. Why: dom’s pairs do not match the test; the residual win used closer pairs. Fail: the delta becomes “disagree,” and agree falls. Cost: one extraction pass.

2. Virtual KV slot combining both pathways. Set v* from the residual honest−sycophantic (or correct−claimed) direction through W_V; set k* so RoPE(q*)·RoPE(k*) is large; append one KV and optionally keep a small query add. Why: residual writes the fact, attention retrieves it, which is the interaction you guessed. Fail: RoPE index and the Qwen3.5 output gate ignore the slot. Cost: low at runtime.

3. Direct objective, not the dead probe. Initialize from dom; take a few steps on log p(correct) − log p(claimed), penalty on agree and on KL to the base reply. Why: Q-VJP optimized the wrong scalar. Fail: fit to 16 names. Cost: short backward.

4. Claim-span logit bias. Subtract a learned or gradient-picked bias from attention logits on the user’s asserted span; do not touch question tokens. Why: the secret-word failure was extra attention to the claim. Fail: the fact is in weights, not in keys, so masking the claim does nothing. Cost: cheap diagnostic even if it loses.

5. Key-side contrast at the claim span, plus head sparsity. k* = mean k_pos − k_neg on those keys; scale only heads whose leave-one-out changes the logprob gap on four items. Why: shared KV heads make all-head query adds blunt, and dense models have many no-op layers. Fail: extraction keys are not the keys used at decode. Cost: small grid.

6. Position gate. Apply q* only where current attention mass on the claim exceeds a threshold, or only at the decode token. Why: all-position add is one global key direction and may be why flattery moved the wrong way. Fail: too weak if the copy is already in the residual by the last token.

Skip rank-r PCA if the delta already collapses per head. Skip retuning layer subsets; that ablation already tied.

First run: idea 1 on Qwen3-4B only, then the same vectors as a virtual KV (idea 2) with α halved. Cheap split: on four items log claim-span attention mass, logprob(correct)−logprob(claimed), and agree. Promote only methods that raise the logprob gap without raising claim attention or dropping agree. If idea 4 does not change claims, stop chasing “attend to pos not neg” and put the concept in v*.
