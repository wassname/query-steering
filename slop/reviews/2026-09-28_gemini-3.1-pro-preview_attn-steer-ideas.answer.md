**Reconstruction and Framing**
You are trying to reduce LLM sycophancy (the tendency to agree with a user’s false claim) by steering the attention mechanism rather than just adding a vector to the residual stream. Your baseline is "dom" (mean-difference query steering), which adds a fixed vector to pre-RoPE queries, effectively creating a single new key-retrieval direction per head. Dom works decently on Qwen3.5 but struggles on Qwen3-4B and smaller models. You want novel attention-based interventions to beat this baseline without sacrificing general coherence.

However, the premise that attention steering *should* beat residual steering is an inference. If residual steering already hits 100% (as observed with the first-token probe), the model already successfully retrieves the correct fact but suppresses it in the residual stream during generation. Attention steering tries to fix a downstream writing problem by forcing an upstream retrieval change. 

**Observations, Inferences, and Missing Facts**
*   **Observations:** Dom works well on Qwen3.5-9B (16/16) but poorly on Qwen3-1.7B (5/16). Q-VJP (gradient-based query shifting targeting a residual concept vector) failed completely (6/16 claims) and correlates poorly with dom. A secret-word dom vector worsened sycophancy.
*   **Inferences:** Dom acts by fetching honest facts over user claims. The failure of Q-VJP suggests that optimizing queries to maximize a residual concept vector creates destructive interference across attention heads.
*   **Missing Facts:** We do not know *which* tokens dom is retrieving (attention weight diffs are missing). We also do not know if Qwen3's dense softmax architecture requires head-specific steering compared to Qwen3.5's sparse softmax architecture.

**Brainstormed Methods**

**1. Value Steering (v-steer)**
Instead of changing *what* the model looks at (queries), change *what it writes* when it looks at a token (values).
*   **Extract:** `v*_L = mean_i( v_L[pos_i, last] - v_L[neg_i, last] )`
*   **Apply:** `v_L[t] += α * v*_L` for all t
*   **Why it works:** Bypasses the complex query-key dot product dynamics. It injects the "honest" concept directly into the output of the attention heads regardless of where the model attends.
*   **Failure mode:** May cause repetition or overwrite distinct factual knowledge since it blindly adds to all values.
*   **Cost:** Low (1 forward pass to extract, standard generation overhead).

**2. Key Steering (k-steer)**
Make the "honest" context globally more attractive to queries.
*   **Extract:** `k*_L = mean_i( k_L[pos_i, instruction_tokens] - k_L[neg_i, instruction_tokens] )`
*   **Apply:** `k_L[t] += α * k*_L`
*   **Why it works:** If sycophancy stems from the user's prompt hijacking attention, boosting the key vectors of the system/honest instructions makes them more "retrievable."
*   **Failure mode:** Could break RoPE dynamics if the added vector interferes with positional encodings.
*   **Cost:** Low.

**3. Head-Masked Dom (q-mask-steer)**
Apply dom only to heads that actually differentiate honest/sycophantic behavior.
*   **Extract:** Calculate `q*_L_h` per head. Keep only heads where `std(pos_i - neg_i)` is lowest (most consistent direction).
*   **Apply:** `q_L_h[t] += α * q*_L_h` (only for top K heads).
*   **Why it works:** Dense softmax models (Qwen3) might suffer when dom shifts queries in heads dedicated to grammar or local context. Masking isolates semantic heads.
*   **Failure mode:** Selecting the wrong heads causes catastrophic degradation.
*   **Cost:** Low to extract, slightly higher engineering cost to apply per-head masks.

**4. Joint Residual & Attention Steering (Res-Q)**
*   **Extract:** Extract both `q*` (dom) and `h*` (residual mean-diff).
*   **Apply:** `q_L[t] += α * q*` AND `h_L[t] += β * h*` (with smaller α, β).
*   **Why it works:** Enhances the retrieval of honest facts (attention) while simultaneously shifting the output probability space (residual). 
*   **Failure mode:** Over-steering leading to rapid coherence loss (high damage).
*   **Cost:** Low.

**What to Try First & Cheap Measurement**
Try **Value Steering (v-steer)** first. It bridges the gap between pure residual steering (which is highly effective) and attention-internal modification. 

To tell these ideas apart cheaply: **Log the Attention Weights (`A_ts`)** during a baseline run versus a dom run. If dom successfully shifts attention weights away from the user's false claim toward the system prompt, query/key steering is the right path. If attention weights remain identical but the output changes, it means dom is accidentally acting like value/residual steering, confirming v-steer or Res-Q is the better approach.
